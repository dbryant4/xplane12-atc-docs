# VFR flight following

**Available now.**

## What it does

A VFR flight to a *different* airport (not [pattern work](vfr-pattern.md)) leaves the
Tower's frequency on its own: climbing through 1,000 ft AGL after takeoff, Tower says
*"frequency change approved"* and hands you nothing further -- from there, radar
services are entirely your call to ask for.

## Requesting it

Ask Departure, Approach, or Center -- whichever's already working you -- for *"flight
following to Portland"* or *"VFR advisories to Portland."* You get a discrete squawk
code right away: *"squawk four five two one."* Asking while still on the ground, or as
an IFR flight, gets *"say again."*

**Radar contact** follows once your transponder actually shows that code, on or in ALT
(not standby) -- *"radar contact, five miles south of Seattle-Tacoma International,
altimeter two niner niner two."* Until then, nothing else changes.

## Handoffs

Once you're being worked, flight following hands you off exactly the way an IFR flight
is handed off -- Departure (or Approach) to Center, then Center to the destination's
Approach as you get close -- including the same *"are you with me?"* reminder if you
don't check in on a new frequency. Each handoff just gets a plain *"roger,"* except the
final one to destination Approach, which adds the altimeter: *"roger, altimeter two
niner niner two."*

## Conformance while following

With no IFR clearance, there's nothing to judge an altitude, heading or speed deviation
against -- so none of the [airborne conformance
rules](conformance-monitor.md#airborne-conformance) apply to a VFR flight at all, until
flight following actually has **radar contact established**. From that point on,
straying off your assigned squawk (or any of the other rules) gets called out exactly
like an IFR flight's would, by whichever controller currently has you. Terminating
service turns it all back off.

## Terminating service

Within 10 nm of your destination, service ends on its own: *"radar service terminated,
squawk VFR, frequency change approved."* Your squawk assignment is cleared -- there's
nothing further to check in for.

## Nearing a Class B, C or D destination

If your route takes you into Class B, C or D airspace, those [entry
rules](vfr-airspace-entry.md) apply independently of flight following -- a Class B
destination still needs its own "request Bravo clearance" call, and Class C/D still just
needs two-way contact.

## Out of scope

- **Sequencing against other traffic.** [Traffic advisories](traffic-advisories.md) are
  built (not yet live -- there's no real X-Plane traffic feed wired up today), but
  sequencing behind another aircraft ("number two, follow the...") isn't -- see the
  [roadmap](../roadmap.md)'s M7-3.
